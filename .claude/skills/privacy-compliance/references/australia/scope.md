# Scope, definitions and exemptions

Read this before flagging anything. A correct finding must apply to an entity the
Act covers, to information the Act treats as personal information, and to
circumstances that are not exempt.

## 1. Who is covered

The Act regulates **APP entities**. An APP entity is an **agency** or an
**organisation** (s 6(1)). Part IIIC (breach notification) and the credit
reporting rules also reach **file number recipients**, **credit reporting
bodies** and **credit providers**.

**Agency** (s 6(1)) — a Minister, a Department, a body or tribunal established
for a public purpose under a Commonwealth law, certain State/Territory bodies in
external Territories, bodies appointed by the Governor-General or a Minister, the
Federal Court, the AFP and similar public bodies.

**Organisation** (s 6C) — an individual, body corporate, partnership,
unincorporated association or trust **that is not**:
- a small business operator (s 6D);
- a registered political party;
- an agency or a State/Territory authority;
- a prescribed instrumentality of a State/Territory.

A legal person can be several different organisations in different capacities
(e.g. personally and as trustee of a trust) — s 6C(2).

**Small business operator** (s 6D) — a business with annual turnover of
**$3,000,000 or less**. This exemption is lost if the operator (s 6D(4)):
- carries on a business with turnover above $3M;
- provides a health service and holds health information (other than in an
  employee record);
- discloses personal information to anyone for a benefit, service or advantage;
- provides a benefit, service or advantage to collect personal information from
  anyone else;
- is a contracted service provider for a Commonwealth contract;
- is a credit reporting body.

## 2. Australian link (s 5B)

The Act extends to acts and practices outside Australia by:
- an **agency** — always (s 5B(1));
- an **organisation or small business operator** — only if it has an
  **Australian link** (s 5B(1A)).

An organisation has an Australian link if it is an Australian citizen, a person
with an unlimited right to remain in Australia, a partnership/trust formed in
Australia, a body corporate incorporated in Australia, or an unincorporated
association centrally managed and controlled in Australia (s 5B(2)) — **or** if it
carries on business in Australia (s 5B(3)).

An act required by an applicable foreign law is not an interference with privacy
(s 13D). Section 5B does not apply to Schedule 2 (s 5B(5)).

## 3. Key definitions (s 6(1))

- **Personal information** — information or an opinion about an identified
  individual, or an individual who is reasonably identifiable, whether or not the
  information is true and whether or not it is recorded in a material form.
- **Sensitive information** — information or an opinion about an individual's:
  racial or ethnic origin; political opinions; membership of a political
  association; religious beliefs or affiliations; philosophical beliefs;
  membership of a professional or trade association; membership of a trade union;
  sexual orientation or practices; or criminal record; **or** health information;
  **or** genetic information that is not otherwise health information; **or**
  biometric information used for automated biometric verification or
  identification; **or** biometric templates.
- **Health information** — includes information about health, disability, the
  provision of health services and related personal information.
- **Government related identifier** — an identifier assigned by an agency, a
  State/Territory authority, their agent, or a Commonwealth/State contracted
  service provider.
- **Employee record** — personal information about an individual that is
  collected and held by an employer in connection with an employment
  relationship (see s 6(1) full definition).
- **Record / de-identified** — de-identification removes personal information
  from the Act's scope only if re-identification is not reasonably possible.

## 4. Exempt acts and practices

### Organisation exemptions (s 7B) — apply via s 7(1)(ee)

- **Non-business individuals** (s 7B(1)) — an individual's acts outside a
  business they carry on.
- **Small-business contractors outside contract** (s 7B(2)) — a contractor for a
  Commonwealth contract acting outside the contract, where it would otherwise be
  a small business.
- **Employee records** (s 7B(3)) — acts or practices **directly related to** a
  current or former employment relationship **and** an employee record held by
  the organisation. This is a common and commonly over-claimed exemption: it does
  not cover recruitment of non-employees, ex-employee data not in an employee
  record, or data shared with third parties.
- **Journalism** (s 7B(4)) — acts in the course of journalism by a media
  organisation that is publicly committed to published privacy standards.
- **State contract** (s 7B(5)) — a contracted service provider acting to meet a
  State contract obligation.

### Political acts and practices (s 7C)

Acts for elections, referendums or participation in the political process by
members of Parliament, local councillors, contractors, subcontractors and
volunteers for registered political parties. Sale or other use/disclosure of
personal information outside those purposes is **not** exempt (Note to s 7C).

### Personal, family or household affairs (s 16)

The APPs do not apply to an individual's collection, holding, use or disclosure
of personal information only for their personal, family or household affairs.

### Related bodies corporate (s 13B)

Collection from, or disclosure to, a related body corporate of personal
information (other than sensitive information) is not an interference with
privacy — but use and holding must still comply with the APPs.

## 5. Permitted general situations (s 16A)

A permitted general situation makes otherwise non-compliant handling lawful
(where the APP clause is expressed to allow it). The seven situations are:

1. Unreasonable or impracticable to obtain consent **and** necessary to lessen or
   prevent a serious threat to life, health or safety (of any individual or
   public health/safety).
2. Reason to suspect unlawful activity or serious misconduct relating to the
   entity's functions, and necessary to take appropriate action.
3. Necessary to help locate a person reported missing (subject to rules).
4. Reasonably necessary for the establishment, exercise or defence of a legal or
   equitable claim.
5. Reasonably necessary for a confidential alternative dispute resolution
   process.
6. Agency's diplomatic or consular functions.
7. Defence Force activities outside Australia (war, peacekeeping, disaster
   relief).

APP 8 (cross-border) and APP 9 (identifiers) **exclude items 4 and 5**.

## 6. Permitted health situations (s 16B)

1. Collection of health information necessary to provide a health service, where
   required/authorised by law or in accordance with binding professional
   confidentiality rules (s 16B(1)).
2. Collection of a third party's family, social or medical history needed to
   provide a health service to a patient (s 16B(1A)).
3. Collection of health information for public-health research, statistics or
   health-service management where de-identified information will not do, consent
   is impracticable and it is under law, professional rules or s 95A guidelines
   (s 16B(2)).
4. Use or disclosure of health information for public-health research or
   statistics under s 95A guidelines (s 16B(3)).
5. Use or disclosure of genetic information to warn a genetic relative of a
   serious threat, under s 95AA guidelines (s 16B(4)).
6. Disclosure of health information to a responsible person when the individual
   cannot consent (s 16B(5)).

## 7. How to use this in a finding

State the entity type, the Australian link basis, the data class, and any
exemption or permitted situation considered — even when you conclude it does not
apply. This makes the finding auditable and prevents the common error of applying
an APP to an exempt small business or an employee record.
