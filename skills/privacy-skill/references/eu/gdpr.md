# EU GDPR — Regulation (EU) 2016/679

General Data Protection Regulation. Full text: [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).
Cite as **GDPR Art N** (e.g. GDPR Art 6(1)(a)), and note recital support where
useful.

## 1. Applicability

- **Material scope (Art 2):** processing of personal data wholly or partly by
  automated means, and non-automated processing of data in a filing system.
  Excludes processing by competent authorities for criminal-law purposes
  (Directive 2016/680), purely personal/household activity, and some Union-law
  activities.
- **Territorial scope (Art 3):** applies to processing in the context of an
  **establishment in the Union**, regardless of where processing occurs (Art 3(1));
  and to non-EU controllers/processors that **offer goods or services** to people
  in the Union or **monitor their behaviour** in the Union (Art 3(2)). Non-EU
  controllers in scope must designate a **representative** (Art 27).
- **Key definitions (Art 4):** personal data (1); processing (2); restriction (3);
  **profiling** (4); pseudonymisation (5); **controller** (7); **processor** (8);
  recipient (9); third party (10); **consent** (11); **personal data breach** (12);
  genetic data (13); biometric data (14); data concerning health (15); main
  establishment (16).

## 2. Principles (Art 5)

Personal data must be:
- (a) processed **lawfully, fairly and transparently**;
- (b) **purpose-limited** — collected for specified, explicit and legitimate
  purposes, not further processed incompatibly;
- (c) **data-minimised** — adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary;
- (d) **accurate** and kept up to date;
- (e) **storage-limited** — kept identifiable no longer than necessary;
- (f) processed with **integrity and confidentiality** (appropriate security).

The controller is responsible for and must be able to **demonstrate** compliance
(**accountability**, Art 5(2)).

## 3. Lawfulness and consent

- **Art 6(1) lawful bases:** consent; contract performance; legal obligation;
  vital interests; public task; legitimate interests (not for public authorities).
  Further processing compatibility is assessed under Art 6(4).
- **Art 7 consent:** the controller must be able to demonstrate consent; it must be
  distinguishable, intelligible and in plain language; **withdrawal must be as easy
  as giving consent**; consent must be freely given (not bundled with contract
  performance where unnecessary).
- **Art 8 children:** information society services offered to a child — consent
  valid from **16** (Member States may lower to **13**); parental consent required
  below that, with reasonable verification efforts.
- **Art 9 special categories (prohibited unless an Art 9(2) condition applies):**
  racial/ethnic origin, political opinions, religious/philosophical beliefs, trade
  union membership, genetic data, biometric data for unique identification, health
  data, sex life or sexual orientation. Conditions include **explicit consent**,
  employment/social-security law, vital interests, not-for-profit bodies,
  manifestly public data, legal claims, substantial public interest, health/medical
  purposes, public health, and research/archiving.
- **Art 10 criminal data:** only under official authority control or authorised by
  law with safeguards.
- **Art 11:** no obligation to keep additional identifying information purely to
  comply; if identification is impossible, tell the data subject and note that
  Art 15–20 rights are limited.

## 4. Data subject rights (Chapter III)

- **Art 12 transparency/modalities:** concise, transparent, intelligible, easily
  accessible, plain language; respond **within one month**, extendable by two
  further months for complex/numerous requests; generally **free of charge**;
  identity may be verified where there are reasonable doubts.
- **Art 13** information where data are collected from the data subject (identity,
  DPO, purposes, legal basis, recipients, transfers, retention, rights, withdrawal,
  automated decisions, whether provision is required).
- **Art 14** information where data were not obtained from the data subject (adds
  categories of data, source); timing rules in Art 14(3).
- **Art 15 right of access** — confirmation and a copy, plus the Art 15(1)
  information and transfer safeguards.
- **Art 16 rectification** and completion of incomplete data.
- **Art 17 erasure ("right to be forgotten")** — grounds in Art 17(1); reasonable
  steps to inform other controllers of published data (Art 17(2)); exceptions in
  Art 17(3).
- **Art 18 restriction of processing.**
- **Art 19** notify recipients of rectification/erasure/restriction.
- **Art 20 data portability** — structured, commonly used, machine-readable;
  applies to consent/contract and automated processing; direct controller-to-
  controller transfer where technically feasible.
- **Art 21 right to object** — to public-task/legitimate-interest processing;
  **absolute right to object to direct marketing** (including related profiling).
- **Art 22 automated decisions** — right not to be subject to solely automated
  decisions with legal or similarly significant effects, subject to Art 22(2)
  exceptions and safeguards (human intervention, express view, contest).
- **Art 23** allows Union/Member State law restrictions.

## 5. Controller and processor obligations (Chapter IV)

- **Art 24** responsibility of the controller — appropriate technical and
  organisational measures, demonstration of compliance, policies.
- **Art 25 data protection by design and by default** — implement principles
  (e.g. minimisation, pseudonymisation) at design time; by default only necessary
  data is processed and not made accessible to an indefinite number of people.
- **Art 26 joint controllers** — determine responsibilities transparently.
- **Art 27 representative** for non-EU controllers/processors in scope.
- **Art 28 processor** — only processors with sufficient guarantees; no
  sub-processors without authorisation; mandatory contract terms (documented
  instructions, confidentiality, Art 32 security, assistance with rights,
  delete/return on termination, audits); sub-processor flow-down; processor
  determining purposes becomes a controller (Art 28(10)).
- **Art 29** processing only on controller instruction.
- **Art 30 records of processing activities** — required contents; a <250-employee
  exemption with exceptions (risk, non-occasional, special categories, criminal
  data).
- **Art 32 security** — risk-appropriate technical and organisational measures,
  including pseudonymisation and encryption, ongoing confidentiality/integrity/
  availability/resilience, restore capability, and regular testing.
- **Art 33 breach notification to supervisory authority** — **without undue delay
  and where feasible within 72 hours** of awareness, unless unlikely to result in a
  risk; content in Art 33(3); phased notification allowed; **document all
  breaches** (Art 33(5)).
- **Art 34 communication to data subjects** — when a breach is likely to result in
  **high risk**; content in Art 34(2); exceptions (encryption rendering data
  unintelligible, subsequent measures, disproportionate effort → public
  communication).
- **Art 35 DPIA** — required for high-risk processing, in particular systematic and
  extensive automated evaluation/profiling, large-scale special-category or
  criminal data, or large-scale systematic monitoring of publicly accessible areas.
- **Art 36 prior consultation** with the supervisory authority where residual risk
  remains high.
- **Art 37–39 DPO** — designation criteria, position and tasks.
- **Art 40–43** codes of conduct and certification as compliance elements.

## 6. Transfers to third countries (Chapter V)

- **Art 44 general principle** — transfers only if Chapter V conditions are met;
  the level of protection must not be undermined (includes onward transfers).
- **Art 45 adequacy decisions.**
- **Art 46 appropriate safeguards** — binding corporate rules (Art 47),
  standard contractual clauses, codes/certification with binding commitments, or
  authorised contractual clauses.
- **Art 48** third-country judgments/decisions enforceable only on an
  international agreement basis (e.g. MLAT).
- **Art 49 derogations** — explicit informed consent, contract necessity, public
  interest, legal claims, vital interests, public registers, and a narrow
  compelling-legitimate-interests route with safeguards and notification.

## 7. Enforcement (Art 83)

Two tiers: up to **EUR 10,000,000 or 2%** of total worldwide annual turnover
(whichever is higher) for obligations such as Art 8, 11, 25–39, 42, 43; up to
**EUR 20,000,000 or 4%** for the basic principles (Art 5, 6, 7, 9), data subject
rights (Art 12–22), transfers (Art 44–49), and non-compliance with supervisory
orders.

## 8. Engineering signals and common failures

| Signal | Check |
|---|---|
| Consent banners / preference centres | Art 7 demonstration, granularity, withdrawal parity; Art 6 basis recorded |
| Special-category data (health, biometrics, ethnicity) | Art 9 condition, not just Art 6; explicit consent where relied on |
| Analytics, profiling, ML, ad tech | Art 5(1)(b) purpose limitation, Art 22 automated decisions, Art 21 objection |
| Data retention jobs / backups | Art 5(1)(e) storage limitation; Art 17 erasure propagation |
| Offshore vendors / regions | Art 44–49 transfer mechanism, Art 28 processor terms, Art 46 safeguards |
| Breach runbooks / alerting | Art 33 72-hour clock and documentation, Art 34 high-risk communication |
| New features / new tech | Art 25 by design and by default, Art 35 DPIA triggers |
| Access/export/delete requests | Art 12 one-month clock, Art 15–20 fulfilment and free-of-charge rule |

**Common failures:** treating "legitimate interests" as a default basis without a
balancing test; non-granular consent and hard-to-withdraw consent; processing
special categories without an Art 9 condition; missing DPIAs for high-risk
features; undisclosed sub-processors and transfers; breach clocks measured from
detection by the wrong team.

## 9. Relationship to the other EU instruments

- The **AI Act** expressly preserves the GDPR (AI Act Art 2(7)); high-risk AI
  deployers may use provider information to run DPIAs (AI Act Art 26(9)).
- The **Data Act** complements GDPR access/portability rights and defers to the
  GDPR on conflict (Data Act Art 1(5)).
